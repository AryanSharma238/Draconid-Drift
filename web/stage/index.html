        How it works

        SPHEREx maps the whole sky every six months in 102 colours of infrared light, from 0.75 to 5 µm. Draconid Drift pulls every SPHEREx exposure that covers the spot you choose, cleans and lines them up, and plays them in time order.

        The bar along the bottom switches between SPHEREx's six detectors, each covering one slice of that range. Every exposure measures one colour at each spot, so measuring a star in all of them gives its spectrum: the dips show water, carbon monoxide or ices.

        Press Compare to put the same patch beside SPHEREx as 2MASS saw it around 2000, blurred to SPHEREx's sharpness. Nearby stars visibly move in between.

        The facts come from SIMBAD and Gaia. Bright sources with no name are sorted into star, galaxy or nebula by a small model trained on SPHEREx sources that SIMBAD has already identified, and are marked as a guess.
