      Targets

      Stars and brown dwarfs close enough to the Sun that SPHEREx sees them move. Pick one to fly there.

        How it works

        SPHEREx maps the whole sky every six months. Draconid Drift pulls every SPHEREx exposure that covers the spot you choose, cleans and lines them up, and plays them in time order.

        To see motion over decades, the left panel shows the same patch from an older survey: 2MASS (about 2000) or AllWISE (2010), blurred to SPHEREx's sharpness so the two compare fairly.

        A star counts as moving when it is clearly detected where it should be now and its old position is empty. Each claim is checked against random spots in the same frames, so the confidence shown is how often noise alone would pass.

        The list beside the images names what is in view from SIMBAD. Bright sources with no name are sorted into star, galaxy or nebula by a small model trained on SPHEREx sources that SIMBAD has already identified.

        Data

        SPHEREx spectral images, NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive.

        2MASS and AllWISE sky maps, served as HiPS by IRSA and CDS.

        Gaia DR3 proper motions, ESA.

        SIMBAD object names and types, CDS Strasbourg (Harvard mirror).

        Built for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, Planet X and SPHEREx.
